Scope note. Research done 2026-11-24. "Project" means the seven-agglomeration pipeline described in `results/RAPORT.md` §16–17, `results/qa/access/access.md` and `results/qa/access/dgregio/dgregio.md` (all read before writing). Project facts are cited to those local files; everything else is cited to a URL. Reading depth is marked: **[full]** = full text read; **[partial]** = full text machine-extracted, only parts readable; **[abstract]** = only abstract, landing page or search summary seen. Inferences, including all recommendations, are kept in the "Inferences" subsections and are mine, not the sources'.

Project baseline used in this note (from `results/RAPORT.md` §17 and `results/qa/access/access.md` §1–2):
- R5 v7.5.1 via r5py 1.1.7; 250 m cells; median travel time over every departure minute 07:00–09:00 on Wednesday 2026-11-25, plus 10–14, 20–22 and Saturday 10–14; access/egress walk ≤ 12 min (1 km) per leg at 5 km/h; min(transit, unlimited walk) rule.
- Performance = population reachable within 30/45 min ÷ population within 7.5/11.25 km straight line (15 km/h) × 100. Origins = populated cells in FUA; destinations and denominator = FUA + 15 km. Headline = population-weighted mean of per-cell ratios; ratio of means shown alongside.
- Core-city results: PT 30 = 23.8 (GZM) to 33.9 (Warszawa); PT 45 = 36.1 (GZM) to 58.8 (Warszawa). Off-peak drops of up to 9.1 points (Trójmiasto evening, 45 min). Population-grid choice moves results by 3–7 points in Kraków, Poznań, Łódź. Transfer limit (max 2 transfers) changes results by ≤ 0.14 points. Median number of transfers in a 200-pair sample is 0 in every city (`access.md` §6).
- Validation: 30 comparable Google Maps pairs, 28 within ±20 %, sum of R5 times = 98 % of planner times (`access.md` §9); DG REGIO reproduction for Warszawa, Poznań, Łódź (`dgregio.md` §1).

## Q1. Families of measures, what the literature recommends, and which project choices are standard or would be questioned

### Takeaway
Cumulative-opportunity measures at a threshold near typical commute time are now explicitly endorsed by recent comparative studies as performing about as well as gravity measures (correlations 0.9–0.97) while being far easier to explain; competition-adjusted measures (2SFCA family) do not predict mode share better and are hard to interpret. The project's core choices (R5, every-minute departures, median, cumulative counts, DG REGIO-style performance ratio) are mainstream, but three choices would draw reviewer questions: the mean-of-ratios headline (DG REGIO and its 2020s successor use ratio of means), the median rather than the "best trip" used in the newer DG REGIO grid study (which makes the project's values not directly comparable), and the absence of any distributional/equity or threshold-curve reporting in the headline.

### Cited Findings

**Cumulative vs gravity**
- Santana Palacios and El-Geneidy (2022, *Findings*) compared 24 cumulative thresholds (1–120 min) with 9 gravity measures for Montreal (GTFS Oct 2016, r5r, 50th percentile, 8–9 AM, census-tract centroids). All cumulative–gravity correlations exceeded 0.9; the maximum, 0.97, occurred when the cumulative threshold equalled the regional mean commute time (48.8 min) paired with log-logistic and Gaussian decay; the four fitted decay curves correlated > 0.97 with each other. Authors recommend a cumulative measure at mean commute time as performing similarly to gravity while being easier to communicate and compute, and for income groups the mean commute time of that group. **[full]** — [Santana Palacios & El-Geneidy 2022](https://findingspress.org/article/32444)
- Kapatsila, Palacios, Grisé and El-Geneidy (2023, *J. Transport Geography* 107, 103530) tested the same question across eight Canadian cities; cumulative and gravity measures correlated at about 0.90 when the cumulative threshold was set at average commute time. **[abstract]** — [Kapatsila et al. 2023](https://iro.uiowa.edu/esploro/outputs/journalArticle/Resolving-the-accessibility-dilemma-Comparing-cumulative/9984701722902771)
- El-Geneidy and Levinson (2006, Minnesota LRRB report) note cumulative measures are easier for the public and planners to understand than gravity measures; a Levinson co-authored paper describes the cumulative measure as a gravity measure with a binary weight. **[abstract]** — [El-Geneidy & Levinson 2006](https://www.lrrb.org/PDF/200616.pdf); [UMN Conservancy](https://conservancy.umn.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/145c266b-2787-4114-b8b8-5f7946e84c7a/content)
- The Transport Access Manual (2020; committee led by David Levinson; CC BY-NC 4.0) has a measure-selection chapter; I did not read its recommendation directly. **[abstract]** — [Transport Access Manual (core.ac.uk)](https://core.ac.uk/download/348843327.pdf); [SSTI summary](https://ssti.us/2020/12/08/new-accessibility-manual-summarizes-data-methods-and-applications/)
- Stępniak, Pritchard, Geurs and Goliszek (2019, *J. Transport Geography* 75: 8–24; Szczecin GTFS) found the cumulative-opportunities measure "highly sensitive" to temporal resolution and "not suited for time-sensitive accessibility analysis", whereas potential (gravity) accessibility was more stable. **[abstract]** — [RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jotrge/v75y2019icp8-24.html); [WTR](https://worldtransitresearch.info/research/7443)

**Competition-adjusted measures (2SFCA and variants)**
- Kapatsila, Negm and El-Geneidy (2026, *JTLU* 19(1): 135–156) compared non-competitive and competition-based job-access measures against census-tract transit mode share in Toronto, Montreal and Vancouver: all were positively associated, but simple cumulative opportunities at the region's mean transit travel time and gravity measures explained mode share better than the more complex competition-based measures, with negligible difference between the first two. **[abstract]** — [Kapatsila, Negm & El-Geneidy 2026](https://jtlu.org/index.php/jtlu/article/view/2832)
- Soukhov, Páez, Higgins and Mohamed (2023, *PLOS ONE* 18(1)) argue that Shen-type accessibility, Weibull accessibility and 2SFCA yield results expressed as a rate whose meaning (opportunities allocated per person) is unclear, show 2SFCA is equivalent to a singly-constrained "spatial availability", and propose spatial availability, which keeps the total number of opportunities fixed and allocates them proportionally to population. Code and data open. **[abstract]** — [PMC full text page](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9858359); [McMaster](https://experts.mcmaster.ca/scholarly-works/2774983); [GitHub](https://www.github.com/soukhova/Spatial-Availability-Measure)
- Pritchard, Tomasiello, Giannotti and Geurs (2019, *Findings*) found that a person-based, competition-adjusted (Shen 1998) potential accessibility shows greater inequality than zonal measures, and that Gini and Palma results depend heavily on which indicator and which assignment method is used; cross-city comparison is valid only with identical method. **[full, except Table 3 values which are an image]** — [Pritchard et al. 2019](https://findingspress.org/article/7412-an-international-comparison-of-equity-in-accessibility-to-jobs-london-sao-paulo-and-the-randstad)

**"Access to destinations" programmes and benchmarks**
- *DG REGIO 2020 (Poelman, Dijkstra, Ackermans, WP 01/2020)*, as established by the project from the source: urban centre 2011 as both origin and destination domain; denominator constrained to the urban centre; OTP 1.2.0, `maxWalkDistance` 5 000 m; 9 departures every 15 min in a 2-hour morning peak, mean of 9 per pair; ratio of means; 42 cities; sample means PT 30 = 24, PT 45 = 57, walk 52, cycle 75. **[full, via project]** — `results/qa/access/dgregio/dgregio.md` §2; `results/RAPORT.md` §9.2; [DG REGIO WP 01/2020 PDF](https://ec.europa.eu/regional_policy/sources/work/012020_low_carbon_urban.pdf)
- *DG REGIO grid follow-up (Poelman & Ackermans, undated, timetables mostly 2019–2021)*: 197 EU urban centres (of 683); origins = populated 200 m cells in the urban centre (JRC 2018 population on a 100 m grid aggregated to 200 m); destinations may lie outside the urban centre; proximity radius **12 km for 45 min and 8 km for 30 min (16 km/h)**; maximum walking 15 min (1.2 km), usable for access, transfer, egress or walk-only; "the best available trip", i.e. **the fastest connection in the morning peak**, is used. Population-weighted averages: **36.2 % (30 min) and 53.9 % (45 min)**. By urban-centre size, 30 min / 45 min averages (min–max): 500k–1m: 39.3 (24.8–56.8) / 59.8 (31.3–79.1); 1m–2.5m: 35.8 (26.0–47.1) / 59.5 (37.4–71.1); 2.5m+: 28.1 (20.4–38.7) / 44.5 (32.4–61.7); 250k–500k: 41.8 / 63.3. Short-trip performance tends to be higher in smaller cities; the size relationship is weaker for 45 min. Polish cities are said to be included ("some major cities in … Czechia and Poland"), but their values appear only in maps, not in extractable text. **[partial]** — [Poelman & Ackermans, "Assessing the performance of urban public transport using population grid data"](https://ec.europa.eu/regional_policy/sources/work/assessing_performance_of_urban_public_transport_using_griddata.pdf)
- *ITF/OECD (2019), Benchmarking Accessibility in Cities* (ITF Policy Paper 68): accessibility, proximity and transport performance computed for 121 European cities in 30 countries for walking, cycling, public transport and car, to destinations including schools, hospitals, food shops, restaurants, people, recreation and green space; dense cities score higher than their commuting zones; car usually beats public transport on door-to-door accessibility even with congestion and parking; averages hide disconnected neighbourhoods (about half of Lisbon commuting-zone residents cannot reach a hospital within 30 min). **[abstract]** — [ITF report page](https://www.itf-oecd.org/benchmarking-accessibility-cities); [Polis summary](https://www.polisnetwork.eu/news/new-itf-report-benchmarking-accessibility-in-cities-measuring-the-impact-of-proximity-and-transport-performance/)
- *UN SDG 11.2.1* (metadata updated 2025-04-23): convenient access = an officially recognised stop within **500 m walking along the street network** of low-capacity transport (bus, BRT) and/or **1 km** of high-capacity transport (rail, metro, ferry); additional criteria include accessibility for special-needs users, **frequent service during peak times** (no numeric threshold given in the text extracted) and safe stops; the city is delineated with the Degree of Urbanisation (endorsed by the UN Statistical Commission, March 2020); indicator to be disaggregated by sex, age and persons with disabilities. **[partial]** — [UN SDG metadata 11.2.1](https://unstats.un.org/sdgs/metadata/files/Metadata-11-02-01.pdf); [UN-Habitat 2020 metadata](https://unhabitat.org/sites/default/files/2020/11/metadata_on_sdg_indicator_11.2.1_2020_1.pdf)
- *ITDP People Near Frequent Transport* (from project's reading of ITDP code): 500 m network walk, mean headway ≤ 10 min, 07:00–21:00, weekday average, GHS-POP 1 km. **[full, via project]** — `results/RAPORT.md` §9.1
- *Access Across America* (University of Minnesota Accessibility Observatory): job accessibility by transit for the largest US metros from GTFS + OSM, reported as worker-weighted average access per census unit; 2022 edition (published Oct 2024, Owen, Liu, Jain, Lind) found year-on-year declines in all top-50 areas. The methodology page returned HTTP 403; I could not confirm departure-window or threshold-weighting details. **[abstract]** — [AAA 2022 data](https://conservancy.umn.edu/items/029d0bf8-e877-4c8b-9e00-9117b0510d07); [CTS news](https://www.cts.umn.edu/news-pubs/news/2024/october/accessibility)

**Thresholds and time-of-day**
- r5r (Pereira et al. 2021, *Findings*): `travel_time_matrix()` computes travel times for every minute of a departure window and returns chosen percentiles (default median); the authors note percentiles reflect service variation over the window but **not schedule deviation absent from GTFS**, and frame the window as addressing the aggregation part of the modifiable temporal unit problem. **[abstract/early sections]** — [r5r Findings](https://findingspress.org/article/21262); [CRAN manual](https://search.r-project.org/CRAN/packages/r5r/r5r.pdf)
- Conway, Byrd and van Eggermond (2018, *JTLU* 11(1): 541–558): transit travel time varies with departure time relative to vehicle arrivals and with transfer alignment; they compute travel time for every departure minute, use percentiles, and for frequency-based scenarios draw several random schedules, then **bootstrap to produce sampling distributions of accessibility, confidence intervals and hypothesis tests between scenarios** (flow chart: random schedules → bootstrap → nth-percentile travel time → sampling distribution → p-value of difference). **[partial: abstract + figure text only]** — [JTLU article](https://jtlu.org/index.php/jtlu/article/view/1074); [UMN Conservancy](https://conservancy.umn.edu/items/820eb79e-364c-4695-957f-54b4739d7a6d)
- Stępniak et al. (2019): resolutions up to 5 min lose negligible precision while cutting computation about fivefold vs 1-min; 15 min gives a good balance, with Gini errors ≤ 0.001; precision loss grows non-linearly as frequency falls. **[abstract]** — [RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jotrge/v75y2019icp8-24.html)
- Murphy and Owen (2019, *JTLU*) on temporal sampling and service-frequency harmonics: repeated calculations across departure times give a more robust picture. **[abstract]** — [UMN Conservancy](https://conservancy.umn.edu/items/e4e77c22-0ecd-4b71-8d18-40df497581d3)

**Project choices as documented**
- The project uses the DG REGIO 2020 radii (7.5 / 11.25 km, 15 km/h), not the 8 / 12 km (16 km/h) of the later grid study; median of every minute, not mean of 9 departures (DG REGIO 2020) or fastest trip (grid study); headline mean of ratios, which `dgregio.md` §2 shows DG REGIO does not use (DG REGIO: ratio of means, confirmed numerically from the annex). — `results/qa/access/access.md` §1; `results/qa/access/dgregio/dgregio.md` §2, §4
- The project's own decomposition: switching aggregation to ratio of means changes core results by +0.5 to +1.6 points in the urban-centre domain; in the urban-centre variant, mean-of-ratios exceeds ratio-of-means by up to 9 points in Trójmiasto (41.2 vs 37.1, 30 min), attributed (unverified) to small denominators at the edge of a small second centre. — `results/RAPORT.md` §17.5; `dgregio.md` §4

### Inferences
- **Standard (a reviewer would accept):** R5 with every-minute departures and median; cumulative population counts; performance ratio à la DG REGIO/ITF; reporting several time windows; showing ratio of means alongside; multiple domains; sensitivity tests (feeds, grid, transfer limit); planner comparison.
- **Would be questioned:**
  1. *Mean of ratios as headline.* It is not what DG REGIO or ITF report, it is sensitive to cells with small denominators (the project already sees this in the urban-centre variant), and it is harder to explain ("average of percentages"). Recommendation: make ratio of means the headline for cross-city comparison and keep mean of ratios as the "typical resident's" figure, or justify the choice explicitly in the report.
  2. *Comparability with DG REGIO grid study.* Its "fastest trip in the peak" and 15-min/1.2 km walk produce systematically higher values than a median with 1 km walk. The project's core 30-min values (24–34) sit below the grid-study class averages for 0.5–2.5 m urban centres (35.8–39.3), but the gap cannot be read as worse service without recomputing with the fastest-trip rule. Recommendation: add a "DG REGIO-grid compatible" variant (urban-centre origins, 8/12 km, 1.2 km walk, 5th-percentile or minimum travel time in 7–9) — R5 can output low percentiles directly — and report it only for comparison.
  3. *Single thresholds (30/45) without a curve.* Given the Canadian evidence that the best single threshold is near mean commute time, and that Polish KBRs report mean trip durations around 23–30 min for all modes (Q2), 30 and 45 min are defensible, but the project already stores 5–60 min thresholds; publishing the full curve (or a time-weighted average across thresholds, in the Access Across America spirit — unverified detail) removes threshold arbitrariness.
  4. *No gravity variant.* Cheap to add from the stored 5-min thresholds (discrete approximation of a negative-exponential or log-logistic decay); main value is robustness, since correlations with cumulative measures are expected to be ≥ 0.9.
  5. *Competition-adjusted measures* are not needed for the headline. If added for jobs, use spatial availability (Soukhov et al.) rather than 2SFCA because it yields an interpretable number (jobs per worker allocated), and present it as a separate, secondary indicator.
- Stępniak et al.'s finding that cumulative measures are sensitive to temporal resolution supports the project's every-minute computation; it does not argue for coarsening.

## Q2. Validation: methods, reported correlations with mode share, and Polish travel-survey figures usable as checks

### Takeaway
Accessibility is usually validated in three ways: travel times against journey planners or observed (AVL/GTFS-RT) times, and accessibility against observed behaviour (mode share, ridership). North American studies consistently find positive associations between transit job accessibility and transit mode share (logit ρ² ≈ 0.6 in Owen & Levinson). For Poland, KBR surveys give modal splits under incompatible definitions and few published trip-time averages; with seven cities a correlation is not meaningful, so KBRs can serve only as a plausibility ordering check.

### Cited Findings

**Travel-time validation**
- Wessel and Farber (2019, *JTLU* 12(1): 475–500): GTFS schedules vs ground truth from archived AVL for four North American agencies over five days; origin-based accessibility, even averaged over one-hour periods, varies widely between locations; in a preprint version, higher-access zones converged on an estimate 5–15 % below the schedule-based value, and low-access origins gave less reliable estimates with more hour-to-hour variability. **[abstract]** — [JTLU](https://jtlu.org/index.php/jtlu/article/view/1502); [RePEc preprint](https://ideas.repec.org/p/osf/socarx/hzgpd.html)
- Wessel, Allen and Farber (2017, *J. Transport Geography* 62: 92–97) built a method to retroactively correct a GTFS feed with real-time vehicle locations (Toronto) and found substantial aggregate accessibility differences between scheduled and observed service. **[abstract]** — [RePEc](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jotrge:v:62:y:2017:i:c:p:92-97)
- Chen and Botta (2026, arXiv 2603.11477, *CEUS*): England-wide correction of bus timetables with UK BODS vehicle positions (May–Oct 2025); for hospital access, 79.3 % of LSOAs had longer observed average travel times than scheduled (79.9 % urban, 76.2 % rural) and 81.6 % had higher observed travel-time variability (86.3 % urban, 57.7 % rural). Their review reports Braga et al. (2023, Fortaleza): median observed times gave accessibility only ~1.5 % below timetable on average, but some areas were off by > 40 % in either direction, and travel-time variability affected accessibility by ~50 % on average. **[full HTML via summary]** — [Chen & Botta 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.11477v1)
- Project: 30 comparable Google Maps pairs, 28 within ±20 %, sum R5/planner = 0.982, median difference −1.9 %; failures explained by departure-minute effects (tram leaving in the query minute). — `results/qa/access/access.md` §9

**Accessibility vs behaviour**
- Owen and Levinson (2015, *Transportation Research A* 74: 110–122): binomial logit of transit vs car commute choice from aggregate area characteristics including continuous (time-weighted) transit job accessibility; ρ² = 0.597. **[abstract]** — [RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/transa/v74y2015icp110-122.html); [UMN](https://conservancy.umn.edu/items/a076cda2-a397-4a00-994b-5b881ddfd92e)
- Foth, Manaugh and El-Geneidy (*TRR* 2417) found increases in transit accessibility augment transit mode share (as cited by later work). **[abstract/secondary]** — [search context: Kapatsila et al.](https://jtlu.org/index.php/jtlu/article/view/2832/1845)
- Kapatsila, Negm and El-Geneidy (2026): all transit job-access measures positively associated with tract-level transit mode share in three Canadian metros; simple cumulative at mean regional transit time and gravity measures fit best. Coefficients not seen. **[abstract]** — [JTLU](https://jtlu.org/index.php/jtlu/article/view/2832)
- Alousi-Jones, Kapatsila, Grisé and El-Geneidy (2024, Edward Elgar chapter) relate transit job-accessibility levels to targeted transit mode shares across eight Canadian metros, aiming at minimum accessibility standards. Values not seen. **[abstract]** — [TRAM McGill PDF](https://tram.mcgill.ca/Research/Publications/TargetModeShares.pdf)

**Polish travel surveys (KBR)**
- Warszawa, WBR 2024–2025 (residents of Warsaw and metropolitan gminas, > 6 years, full working day): trips ending in the clean transport zone (SCT, centre): PT 40.6 %, walk 33 %, car 23.6 %; outside SCT: walk 37.3 %, car 35.5 %, PT 23.6 %; metropolitan gminas: trips not crossing Warsaw border PT 6.2 %, trips ending in Warsaw PT 20.7 %, into the SCT almost 40 %; 23 % of working residents remote/hybrid. No city-wide modal split, trip duration or sample size on the page. — [warszawa19115.pl](https://warszawa19115.pl/-/warszawskie-badanie-ruchu)
- Kraków, KBR 2023 (May–June 2023 household survey, 10 000 households / 21 087 respondents, 6 000 in Kraków + 35 gminas): PT 54 %, car 39 %, bicycle 5 % **of non-walking trips**; mean trip duration of Kraków residents **almost 30 min**; 1.92 trips per person per weekday (~1.5 m trips/day). Not stated whether the split covers only Kraków residents. — [BIP Kraków](https://www.bip.krakow.pl/?sub_dok_id=184386)
- Wrocław, KBR 2024 (~10 500 surveys, Wrocław + 9 gminas, > 6 years, trips ≥ 100 m, weekday, walking included): car 39.8 % (2018: 41.4), walk 33.1 % (24.2), **PT 21.6 % (27.6)**, bicycle 4.7 % (6.3), e-scooter 0.8 %; 1.7 trips/person/day; **mean trip duration ~23 min**; PT trips grew by 8 300 in absolute terms despite share fall. — [wroclaw.pl](https://www.wroclaw.pl/komunikacja/kompleksowe-badanie-ruchu-2024-we-wroclawiu-i-gminach-sasiednich-wyniki)
- Poznań, KBR 2018 (synthesis Nov 2019): PT 33.7 % of all trips of Poznań residents (42.4 % of non-walking); 2013: 42.4 % (49.0 %); agglomeration-wide all traffic: walk 16.0 %, bike 7.1 %, PT 25.4 %, car 51.5 %; 2.20 trips/person/day. New KBR in progress (field 2026–2027). — `results/qa/phase0/research/kbr_modal_split.md` (from the city PDF, pp. 9–11); [ZTM Poznań news](https://www.ztm.poznan.pl/aktualnosci/pozostale/w-poznaniu-i-metropolii-rozpoczely-sie-kompleksowe-badania-ruchu/)
- Gdańsk, GBR 2022/23: PT 28.7 %, car 42.8 %, walk 24.2 %, bike + scooter 4.3 % (2016: PT 32.1 %); secondary source (trojmiasto.pl), report not opened. — `kbr_modal_split.md`
- Łódź: Łódzkie Badanie Ruchu started October 2026 (results not yet available); earlier survey not identified. GZM: no household travel survey with modal split found (this session's search of BIP/InfoGZM returned only workshop and SUMP documents). — `kbr_modal_split.md`; [search result: GZM SUMP 2023 listing](https://bip.metropoliagzm.pl/attachments/download/193334)
- Radio Kraków reports an average PT trip of "17 min 41 s" for Kraków, origin and method unknown (likely app data, not KBR). **[secondary, unverified]** — [Radio Kraków](https://www.radiokrakow.pl/aktualnosci/krakow/raport-o-komunikacji-miejskiej-krakow-chwalony-za-czestotliwosc-kursow-krytykowany-za-opoznienia-autobusow/)

**Open real-time data in Poland (for a scheduled-vs-observed check)**
- GTFS-RT vehicle positions are listed for ZTM Gdańsk (CC BY 4.0) and ZTP Kraków (bus and tram) on Transitland; Warsaw positions are available via a third-party GTFS-RT built from the city API (api.um.warszawa.pl, API key required; trip IDs may not match static GTFS); no public multi-month archive found; Poznań RT feed not confirmed in this search. — [Transitland Gdańsk RT](https://www.transit.land/feeds/f-u3tm-ztm~gdansk~rt); [Transitland Kraków RT](https://www.transit.land/feeds/f-ztp~krakow~bus~rt); [mkuran.pl GTFS](https://mkuran.pl/gtfs)

### Inferences
- **What the project's planner check does and does not show.** ±20 % agreement on 30 pairs validates the network build, not accessibility: Google also routes on schedules. It cannot detect the schedule-vs-reality bias (5–15 % overstatement in Wessel & Farber; widespread longer observed times in England).
- **Recommended validation steps (in order of cost):**
  1. Enlarge and stratify the planner sample (e.g. 100 pairs, random population-weighted origins, include off-peak and Saturday) and report the distribution of R5/planner ratios, not just the share within ±20 %. Compare R5 median-over-window with planner results averaged over several query minutes to remove the "tram leaves in the query minute" artefact.
  2. Collect one to two weeks of GTFS-RT vehicle positions for Gdańsk and Kraków (and Warszawa via the city API) for the reference window, build an "observed" GTFS (Wessel et al. 2017 method, or Chen & Botta's matching), rerun R5 for those cities only, and publish the scheduled-minus-observed performance gap by area, separately for tram/rail-served and bus-only cells. This turns limitation 16.2.1 into a measured number.
  3. Behavioural plausibility: within-city, not between-city. KBR zone-level PT shares (if the cities release zone tables, e.g. Warszawa SCT vs non-SCT; Wrocław, Kraków model zones) can be correlated with zone-mean performance; between cities, n = 7 with incompatible definitions supports only a rank comparison. Report it as description, as the project brief already does.
  4. Ridership: stop-level boardings (e.g. Kraków October 2023 counts, Wrocław stop counts in KBR 2024) against stop-catchment accessibility, if the authorities release them.
- **KBR figures as a threshold check.** Mean all-mode trip durations of ~23 min (Wrocław) and ~30 min (Kraków) imply mean PT trips somewhat longer (PT trips are typically longer than walk trips — inference, not sourced), so the 30-min threshold is near the "mean commute time" that Santana Palacios & El-Geneidy recommend, and 45 min is a generous upper bound.
- Mode-share comparisons must use one definition (all trips incl. walking, residents of the core city); currently only Wrocław 2024, Poznań 2018 and Gdańsk 2022/23 qualify, and they rank Poznań (33.7) > Gdańsk (28.7) > Wrocław (21.6), whereas project PT 45 ranks Trójmiasto 55.4 > Poznań 54.6 > Wrocław 53.0 — differences within noise of both measures.

### Gaps
- Warszawa WBR 2024/25 city-wide modal split, trip times and sample not published on the city page; full report not found.
- No GZM household travel survey found; Łódź survey only started.
- Owen & Levinson elasticities, Kapatsila et al. coefficients and Alousi-Jones target values not read (abstracts only).
- No Polish study found that correlates accessibility with mode share or ridership.
- Whether Poznań publishes GTFS-RT not confirmed; no public RT archive for any Polish city found.

## Q3. Equity and distribution, and what is feasible without socio-economic microdata

### Takeaway
Current transport-justice literature favours (a) inequality measures that compare groups (Palma ratio, concentration index) over the Gini, and (b) sufficientarian "accessibility poverty" measures with an explicit, normatively chosen threshold, summarised with Foster–Greer–Thorbecke (FGT) indices. Gap analyses (Currie's needs gap, Jiao's transit deserts) compare supply with need. Without income data the project can still report distributions, poverty-line headcounts, age-group-specific access (census age on a grid) and needs-gap maps using age and car-free proxies.

### Cited Findings
- Karner, Pereira and Farber (*Transportation*, online 2024, vol. 52(4): 1399–1427, 2025) review Gini/Lorenz approaches (inequality) and needs-gap/transit-desert approaches (accessibility poverty); they argue the theoretical foundations of these methods conflict with egalitarian and sufficientarian views of transport justice and that their assumptions "severely limit" their utility, illustrated with Washington D.C. Covid-19 service cuts; they propose alternatives. **[abstract]** — [IPEA page](https://www.ipea.gov.br/acessooportunidades/en/publication/2024_transp_measuring_transportation_equity/); [RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/p/osf/socarx/y246u.html)
- IPEA accessibility workshop (2024): Palma ratio = mean access of richest 10 % ÷ poorest 40 % (Belo Horizonte example 3.8); concentration index (−1 to 1; Belo Horizonte corrected CI 0.335, pro-rich); advises "you probably shouldn't use the Gini" citing Karner et al.; accessibility poverty line is a moral/political choice (example: 25th percentile of access ≈ 23 000 jobs); FGT0 headcount (example 14.8 %), FGT1 poverty gap (4.9 %), FGT2 severity (2.9 %). **[full]** — [IPEA workshop, equity measures](https://ipeagit.github.io/access_workshop_eit_2024/3.3_equity_measures.html)
- Pereira, Schwanen and Banister (2017, *Transport Reviews* 37(2): 170–191) is the standard justice framework reference (cited by IPEA). **[not read]** — [IPEA workshop references](https://ipeagit.github.io/access_workshop_eit_2024/3.3_equity_measures.html)
- Pritchard et al. (2019): Gini depends on indicator and assignment method; population-weighted methods (assigning each resident their zone's value) preferred over unweighted zone distributions; in São Paulo transit is more unequal than car (Palma), while in the Randstad lowest-income groups have better transit access (more central). **[full text, values not seen]** — [Findings](https://findingspress.org/article/7412-an-international-comparison-of-equity-in-accessibility-to-jobs-london-sao-paulo-and-the-randstad)
- Giannotti et al. (2021, *Cities* 109, 103016): larger transit accessibility inequality in São Paulo than London, using Lorenz/Gini and occupation as socio-economic proxy. **[abstract]** — [UCL preprint](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/10149347/1/Giannotti_TransitAccessEquitySaoPauloLondon_preprint2.pdf)
- Goliszek (2026, *Geographia Polonica* 99(1): 5–33), Szczecin: cumulative access to kindergartens, primary and secondary schools and jobs, 5–60 min in 5-min steps, PT from GTFS (October 2018), car from Google Maps API; **population groups by age (3–6, 7–15, 16–18, 19–65) matched to trip purposes**; Lorenz curves and Gini; car better in extent and equity; largest disparities in the 20–30 min band; within 20 min central zones reach ~50–60 % of workplaces vs 10–20 % elsewhere; PT Gini higher than car, largest gap for workplaces. **[partial]** — [Geographia Polonica](https://www.geographiapolonica.pl/author/id/3528.html); [RCIN PDF](https://rcin.org.pl/igipz//Content/253445/WA51_290427_r2026-t99-z1_G-Polonica-Goliszek.pdf)
- Currie (2010, *J. Transport Geography* 18(1): 31–41; earlier ATRF 2007), Melbourne: measures spatial distribution of PT supply against a composite index of transport need and identifies spatial gaps; a 2024 RMIT application measures horizontal equity (supply per person) and vertical equity (socio-economic index) and finds little relation between supply and disadvantage. Exact needs formula not read. **[abstract]** — [RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jotrge/v18y2010i1p31-41.html); [RMIT 2024](https://www.rmit.edu.au/content/dam/rmit/au/en/research/networks-centres-groups/cur/longitudinal-analysis-working-paper-final-april-2024.pdf)
- Jiao and Dillivan (2013, *J. Public Transportation* 16(3): 23–39): transit desert = area where transit supply falls short of the demand of transit-dependent population; GIS method at block-group level; later Texas work uses Gap = z(supply) − z(demand); CBDs are almost always "transit oases". **[abstract]** — [USF digital commons](https://digitalcommons.usf.edu/jpt/vol16/iss3/2); [Texas report](https://sites.utexas.edu/cm2/wp-content/uploads/sites/4241/2019/04/Year2_JJ_TransitDeserts.pdf)
- Rosik and Duma (2025, *Geographia Polonica* 98(3)): first Polish assessment of areas at risk of transport poverty at municipal level, combining PT demand and supply (rail and bus). **[abstract]** — [RCIN](https://rcin.org.pl/igipz//Content/246179/WA51_282864_r2025-t98-no3_G-Polonica-Rosik.pdf)
- SDG 11.2.1 asks for disaggregation by sex, age and disability. **[partial]** — [UN metadata](https://unstats.un.org/sdgs/metadata/files/Metadata-11-02-01.pdf)
- GUS publishes NSP 2021 results on a 1 km grid for the variables of Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2018/1799 through the Geostatistical Portal and BDL (staged loading June–August 2023). Regulation 2018/1799 grid variables are, to my knowledge, total population, sex, age bands (< 15, 15–64, ≥ 65), employed persons, place of birth and residence one year earlier; this list is from memory and was **not verified** this session. — [GUS publication schedule](https://stat.gov.pl/spisy-powszechne/nsp-2021/harmonogram-publikacji-wynikow-nsp-2021)

### Inferences
- **Feasible now, without new data:**
  1. *Distribution of per-cell performance* (population-weighted deciles, share of residents below a threshold) for each city — this is the sufficientarian view and is cheap. Example: share of core-city residents who reach < 20 % of nearby population within 45 min; FGT0/FGT1/FGT2 against that line. Pick the line transparently (e.g. a fixed absolute line, or 50 % of the city median) and show sensitivity.
  2. *Group-based access by age.* Using the GUS 1 km grid (age bands, if confirmed) disaggregated to 250 m in proportion to total population, compute population-weighted performance for children, working-age and 65+ residents; report the ratio of 65+ to working-age access (a Palma-like ratio on age instead of income). Caveat: within-km-cell age distribution assumed uniform.
  3. *Needs-gap map (Currie/Jiao style):* need = z-score of share 65+ and under 15 (and, if available, car-free households from NSP 2021 at gmina/district level), supply = z-score of PT performance or frequent-service share; map gap = supply − need. Label it a screening tool.
  4. *Avoid a headline Gini* (Karner et al.; IPEA). If a Gini is shown for comparability with Goliszek (2026) or Stępniak et al., compute it population-weighted (Pritchard et al. method 2) and say what it can't show.
  5. *Deprivation proxies:* municipal-level indicators (e.g. GUS BDL unemployment, social-assistance recipients per 1 000) are available for gminas and Warsaw districts only; they could rank districts, not cells. Rosik & Duma's municipal transport-poverty work is the nearest Polish precedent.
- The project's existing "density × frequency" layer is already a supply-vs-potential-demand map; re-framing it as a needs gap with age weights would be a small step.

### Gaps
- Exact content of the NSP 2021 1 km grid on Geoportal GUS (age bands, availability at 250 m) not verified.
- No Polish cell-level deprivation index found in this session.
- Currie's needs-index formula and Karner et al.'s proposed alternatives not read.
- Palma and Gini values from Pritchard et al. Table 3 not available as text.

## Q4. Comparable results for Polish and Central European cities

### Takeaway
Published Polish GTFS-based accessibility work is mostly single-city (Szczecin dominates: Goliszek, Stępniak, Połom, Duma) and uses jobs or services, not population performance, so direct numeric placement of the project's values is possible only against DG REGIO (three Polish cities) and the EU-wide averages. The project's values are of the same order as DG REGIO 2020 and below the DG REGIO grid-study averages, with method differences sufficient to explain the gap.

### Cited Findings
- DG REGIO 2020 annex values (exact): Warszawa PT 30 = 30.9, PT 45 = 61.7, walk 30 = 49.0, cycle 30 = 70.4; Poznań 26.3 / 74.8 / 43.0 / 81.4; Łódź 25.4 / 74.8 / 54.3 / 81.1; 42-city means PT 30 = 24, PT 45 = 57, walk 52, cycle 75. — `results/qa/access/dgregio/dgregio.md` §1; `results/RAPORT.md` §9.2
- Project's reproduction in the DG REGIO domain and method: Warszawa 37.1 / 66.0, Poznań 36.1 / 70.3, Łódź 31.7 / 63.8 (PT 30 / PT 45); residuals (DG − project) −6.2 / −4.3, −9.8 / +4.5, −6.3 / +11.0. — `dgregio.md` §1
- DG REGIO grid follow-up (Poelman & Ackermans; 197 urban centres; fastest trip; 8/12 km): population-weighted means 36.2 % (30 min) and 53.9 % (45 min); 1m–2.5m class 35.8 % (26.0–47.1) and 59.5 % (37.4–71.1); 2.5m+ class 28.1 % and 44.5 %. — [Poelman & Ackermans](https://ec.europa.eu/regional_policy/sources/work/assessing_performance_of_urban_public_transport_using_griddata.pdf) **[partial]**
- Goliszek, Połom and Duma (2020, *Bulletin of Geography. Socio-economic Series* 50: 133–146): potential and cumulative accessibility of workplaces by PT in Szczecin from a GTFS model; potential accessibility for the 7–9 AM peak, cumulative at 08:00 for 10–60 min thresholds; numbers not on landing page. **[abstract]** — [BGSS](https://apcz.umk.pl/BGSS/article/view/30818)
- Goliszek (2026), Szczecin: within 20 min by PT, central zones reach ~50–60 % of the city's workplaces, most other districts 10–20 %; 60 min gives near-complete coverage; PT Gini > car Gini. **[partial]** — [RCIN PDF](https://rcin.org.pl/igipz//Content/253445/WA51_290427_r2026-t99-z1_G-Polonica-Goliszek.pdf)
- Stępniak et al. (2019), Szczecin, temporal-resolution study (see Q1). **[abstract]** — [RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jotrge/v75y2019icp8-24.html)
- Rosik et al. (2018, *Transport*): gmina-level bus accessibility in Poland; Rosik & Duma (2025) municipal transport poverty. National/regional, not intra-urban. **[abstract]** — [RCIN Rosik 2025](https://rcin.org.pl/igipz//Content/246179/WA51_282864_r2025-t98-no3_G-Polonica-Rosik.pdf)
- A Warsaw grocery-access study (2021, other authors) found car advantage at all thresholds off-peak but transit advantage at 15 min in the rush hour. **[search summary only; authors not identified]** — [search context, IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jotrge/v96y2021ics0966692321002131.html)
- ITDP Atlas: Prague and Wrocław reported at the top of the PNFT ranking (secondary source); percentages not retrieved. — `results/RAPORT.md` §9.1

### Inferences
- **Placing the project's numbers.** Core-city PT 45 of 51–59 (excluding GZM) is close to the DG REGIO 2020 42-city mean (57) and to the grid study's 1m–2.5m class mean (59.5). Core PT 30 of 27–34 is above the DG REGIO 2020 mean (24) but below the grid-study class means (35.8–39.3); the grid study's "fastest trip" and 1.2 km walking allowance both raise values relative to a median with 1 km walk (the project measured +0.4 to +1.7 points for 5 km walk and −1.1 to −1.7 for mean-of-9-departures vs median; the fastest-trip effect is not measured but should be larger, since the minimum over a 2-hour window removes almost all waiting). GZM (36 at 45 min) falls near the minimum of the 1m–2.5m class (37.4) and the 2.5m+ class mean (44.5) — plausibly a polycentric-structure effect, consistent with the project's own frequent-service results.
- For Polish comparisons beyond DG REGIO, the realistic option is to compute Goliszek-style indicators (share of city jobs reachable within 20/30 min, Gini) from the project's own jobs layer (Goliszek dataset already used), which would allow a like-for-like reference to Szczecin.

## Q5. Known error sources: scheduled vs real time, incomplete feeds, MAUP, edge effects, study-area size

### Takeaway
The best-documented bias is schedule optimism: observed-service accessibility is typically 5–15 % lower in well-served areas (Toronto) and observed travel times are longer for about 80 % of small areas in England, with much larger local errors either way. Boundary choice changes absolute accessibility values strongly and relative ones less, and edge bias is largest for long-range, distance-cumulative measures — exactly what the project's own DG REGIO decomposition found (domain explains most of a 20-point gap).

### Cited Findings
- Schedule vs observed: Wessel & Farber 2019 (5–15 % lower in high-access zones; low-access origins unreliable) **[abstract]** — [JTLU](https://jtlu.org/index.php/jtlu/article/view/1502); Chen & Botta 2026 (79.3 % of LSOAs longer observed hospital travel time; 81.6 % higher variability; urban variability more affected) and Braga et al. 2023 via Chen & Botta (mean −1.5 %, local errors > 40 %, variability effect ~50 %) — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.11477v1)
- r5r authors: travel-time percentiles over a window reflect timetable variation only, not deviations from schedule. — [r5r Findings](https://findingspress.org/article/21262)
- Boundaries: a Harris County bus-network study found tighter analysis boundaries give higher absolute accessibility values, while relative measures (percentage change between scenarios) were consistent across boundary sizes; a follow-up on two transit projects found relative measures inconsistent between projects; authors advise always reporting the boundary. **[abstract]** — [RePEc WP "analysisboundaries"](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:nex:wpaper:analysisboundaries)
- Edge effects: Porter et al.-type simulation (2010, *Int. J. Health Geographics* 9:40) found considerable edge bias for accessibility measures, smaller for availability and short-range measures; censoring increases with buffer size; recommends guard areas and avoiding large-range distance-based measures. **[abstract]** — [Int J Health Geogr](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/1476-072X-9-40); [PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC2915963/)
- MAUP/temporal unit: r5r frames departure windows as addressing the modifiable temporal unit problem — [r5r Findings](https://findingspress.org/article/21262); Montreal studies show misestimation depends on spatial/temporal unit choice — **[search summary]** [McGill eScholarship](https://escholarship.mcgill.ca/downloads/79408385k)
- Poelman & Ackermans note that timetable data are not available for the same timeframe across cities and are patchier in some regions, which skews the analysed set. **[partial]** — [Poelman & Ackermans](https://ec.europa.eu/regional_policy/sources/work/assessing_performance_of_urban_public_transport_using_griddata.pdf)
- Project findings on its own error sources: domain change explains +6.5 to +14.5 points of PT 45 (`dgregio.md` §1); population grid 3–7 points (`RAPORT.md` §17.3); feed set ≤ 0.5 points in cores; transfer limit ≤ 0.14; 5–21 % of FUA population outside GTFS coverage; 28 of 42 feeds unofficial; one internal consistency check failing in up to 730 cells (`RAPORT.md` §16.2, §17.4); in a small urban centre, mean of ratios inflated by small edge denominators (`RAPORT.md` §17.5).

### Inferences
- **Scheduled vs real.** Applying Wessel & Farber's 5–15 % to the project would lower PT 45 by roughly 3–9 points in cores, comparable to between-city differences in the middle of the table; it is likely larger for bus-dependent and polycentric areas (GZM) than for tram/rail corridors. This is an inference; only a GTFS-RT run (Q2) can quantify it for Polish cities. A cheap proxy is a sensitivity run with bus segments slowed by a fixed factor (e.g. scheduled speed × 0.9 in mixed traffic) — clearly labelled as hypothetical.
- **Edge effects.** The project's FUA + 15 km destination buffer is a correct guard area for 45-min transit (11.25 km radius); edge effects therefore mostly concern origins near the FUA boundary, where both numerator and denominator shrink — already handled by the ratio, but mean-of-ratios amplifies noise where denominators are small. Recommendation: report the share of population in cells with denominator below a floor (e.g. < 10 000 people) and either drop or flag them.
- **Study-area size.** The urban-centre variant shows the ratio converging to "share of the whole centre reachable" when the centre is small relative to the 11.25 km radius. Recommendation: add an area-independent variant — e.g. performance computed with a fixed set of destinations defined by the radius only (no domain clipping), or report "population reachable within 45 min as a share of the population within 11.25 km, all cells regardless of boundary", for every origin, then aggregate over a common domain (urban centre 2021). The project's production setup already does this for the denominator; the remaining issue is the origin domain, which should be identical in kind (e.g. urban centre) for between-city comparison.
- **MAUP.** 250 m cells are fine relative to the 1 km walk; the larger MAUP risk is population disaggregation (the census-vs-GHSL 3–7 point swing). Recommendation: treat grid choice as an uncertainty band in the headline (report both, or a range), since it exceeds several between-city gaps.
- **Incomplete feeds.** The project's checks (operator line lists, vehicle-km vs annual reports, OSM stop matching) go beyond what most academic papers do. A missing piece is destination-side incompleteness; a test is to compare reachability of cells served only by non-GTFS operators (OSM stops with no GTFS match) — the project already computes these stop matches.

### Gaps
- No Polish scheduled-vs-observed accessibility study found.
- No study found that tests grid-cell resolution specifically for transit accessibility.
- Liu et al. (2023) "travel time inaccuracy" and Javanmard et al. (2025) numbers not seen.

## Q6. Outputs that add most for a non-specialist reader

### Takeaway
Programmes aimed at the public (Access Across America, ITF, SDG 11.2.1, ITDP) report simple counts or shares tied to a threshold and services people recognise (jobs, hospitals, schools); research tools (Conveyal, r5r) make percentile bands and departure-time variation easy to compute. For lay readers the evidence-backed additions are destination-specific access (hospitals, secondary schools), time-of-day curves and a reliability/variation band, plus interactive isochrones.

### Cited Findings
- ITF 2019 reports accessibility to schools, hospitals, food shops, restaurants, green space and people, and highlights a concrete lay statistic: about half of Lisbon commuting-zone residents cannot reach a hospital within 30 min. **[abstract]** — [ITF](https://www.itf-oecd.org/benchmarking-accessibility-cities)
- ITF also published "Benchmarking Accessibility to Services Across Cities" (follow-up page). **[not read]** — [ITF services page](https://www.itf-oecd.org/benchmarking-accessibility-services-across-cities)
- Goliszek (2026) uses service-specific access matched to age groups (kindergartens 3–6, primary 7–15, secondary 16–18, jobs 19–65) and threshold bands 5–60 min. **[partial]** — [Geographia Polonica](https://www.geographiapolonica.pl/author/id/3528.html)
- Chen & Botta (2026) report hospital access by small area with observed variability, showing feasibility of reliability metrics. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.11477v1)
- r5r and Conveyal compute multiple percentiles of travel time over a departure window (e.g. 5th, 25th, 50th, 75th, 95th), which directly supports "best case / typical / bad case" bands. — [r5r Findings](https://findingspress.org/article/21262); Conway et al. 2018 [JTLU](https://jtlu.org/index.php/jtlu/article/view/1074)
- Access Across America publishes per-census-unit job access tables and rankings for metros. — [AAA 2022 data](https://conservancy.umn.edu/items/029d0bf8-e877-4c8b-9e00-9117b0510d07)
- In Poland, school registry (RSPO, rspo.gov.pl) is searchable and exportable to CSV; spatial location of schools is provided from SIO databases under the education information act. API/coordinates availability not confirmed. — [Kuratorium Warszawa](https://www.kuratorium.waw.pl/pl/rodzice-i-uczniowie/rejestr-szkol-i-placowe/14386,Rejestr-szkol-i-placowek-oswiatowych.pdf); [Ustawa o SIO 2021](https://api.sejm.gov.pl/eli/acts/DU/2021/584/text/T/D20210584L.pdf)

### Inferences (prioritised recommendations, mine)
1. **Isochrone from a clicked point** (30/45 min, peak vs evening, with the 25th–75th percentile band shaded). Highest value per effort for lay readers; precomputation is impossible at 250 m for all cells, so either serve on demand from a small R5 server or precompute for stop clusters/centroids of 1 km cells.
2. **Travel time to a chosen destination** (city centre node, main station, nearest hospital with an emergency department, nearest university): one-to-all maps are cheap (one R5 run per destination) and answer "how long from my area".
3. **Access to named services:** share of residents with an emergency hospital (SOR) within 30 min and a secondary school within 20/30 min by PT, by city and district; matched to age groups from the census grid (Goliszek-style). Data: RSPO/SIO for schools, the national registry of healthcare providers for hospitals (registry access not checked this session), OSM as fallback.
4. **Accessibility by time of day as a curve** (e.g. 05:00–24:00 in 30-min steps, Wednesday and Saturday) rather than four windows; the project already found city-specific evening collapses (Trójmiasto −9 points) that a curve communicates better than a table.
5. **Reliability band:** show performance at the 25th and 75th percentile of travel time alongside the median (R5 returns these at no extra routing cost), and, once available, a scheduled-vs-observed band for cities with GTFS-RT.
6. **Plain-language indicators:** "people (or jobs) you can reach in 30 minutes" is already shown with a size caveat; add "% of the people within 7.5 km that you can reach in 30 minutes" phrasing and a comparison with walking and cycling on the same scale, which the project already computes.
7. **Distribution graphic:** population-weighted histogram or decile bars per city, with an explicit "accessibility poverty" line (Q3), instead of a single average.
8. **Uncertainty statement next to the headline:** grid choice (±3–7 points), schedule optimism (unmeasured; literature 5–15 %), boundary definition — as a short range rather than a footnote.

### Gaps
- No user-testing evidence found on which accessibility visualisations lay audiences understand best.
- Availability and licence of Polish hospital registry coordinates and RSPO API not verified.
- Conveyal methodology documentation page returned 404; Conveyal's own guidance on percentile reporting not read.
