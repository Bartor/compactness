## 1. Travel surveys (KBR) per agglomeration: modal split, trip times, transfers

### Takeaway
Usable household-survey benchmarks exist for Wrocław 2024, Gdańsk 2022/23, Kraków 2023 and Poznań 2018. Warszawa 2024/25 has been published only as partial shares on a city page. Łódź (started October 2026) and Poznań (started autumn 2026) are still in the field, and no GZM-wide household survey was found. The surveys define a trip differently (≥100 m vs ≥250 m), define the base differently (all trips vs non-walking trips) and cover different areas. Only the "all trips, walking included" figures for Wrocław, Gdańsk and Poznań can be compared, and only roughly.

### Inferences
- **Differences in method that limit comparison:** (a) trip threshold: Wrocław ≥100 m, Gdańsk ≥250 m, so Wrocław counts more short walks; (b) base: Kraków publishes only non-walking shares; Poznań gives both, and they differ by about 9 points (33.7 vs 42.4); (c) Gdańsk counts every mode used in a trip, while the others use the main mode; (d) area: city residents only vs residents of neighbouring gminas, and Warszawa reports by destination zone; (e) years range from 2018 to 2025, and the 2018 Poznań and 2015 Warsaw values predate COVID and remote work (23% of Warsaw workers are remote or hybrid).
- **Most useful direct check of the project's results:** Gdańsk's mean PT trip of **36 min** against car 25 min (ratio about 1.4), together with Kraków's "almost 30 min" mean trip, puts the project's 30- and 45-minute thresholds in context: the typical surveyed PT trip in Gdańsk takes longer than 30 minutes.
- Surveys do not yet support a "share of PT trips with a transfer" check, except Gdańsk's ~6% multimodal trips (all modes). Jakdojade's 29–42% of journeys with at least one transfer is much higher, because it covers app users planning PT journeys (a selected population) rather than all residents. It is the closest available benchmark for the project's transfer-ease layer; the order (Warszawa has the most transfers, Trójmiasto and GZM the fewest) is worth comparing with the project's stop-area transfer results.
- Satisfaction and reasons for mode choice: none of the opened pages gives numbers. Wrocław publishes them only as infographics, and Kraków's P+R survey says users choose P+R to save cost and avoid congestion.

### Gaps
- Warsaw WBR 2024/25 full report: no city-wide modal split, trip times or transfer shares found. The page [um.warszawa.pl WBR article](https://um.warszawa.pl/-/warszawskie-badanie-ruchu-czyli-jak-przemieszczaja-sie-mieszkancy-stolicy-i-metropolii-) was not opened.
- Kraków KBR 2023 survey report (BIP attachment `/zalaczniki/dokumenty/n/457396/karta`) not opened; walking share, per-mode times and transfers unknown.
- Wrocław full KBR 2024 report not opened; per-mode trip times unknown.
- Łódź: no previous KBR found (searches for a 2013 study found nothing). GZM: no household survey found.
- Gdynia and Sopot: no survey verified.

## 2. Operator and authority statistics; national compilations (IGKM, GUS, BDL)

### Takeaway
City-level operator statistics exist (Warsaw ZTM annual report, Wrocław city summaries, MPK Poznań annual reports), but they are scattered and use different definitions. GUS's BDL has an urban-transport subject tree (stops, bus lanes, P&R, passengers, fleet and operations) that can be queried by API, while GUS's "Transport – wyniki działalności" does not publish city-level urban-transport figures on its landing page. Independent timetable-based tram-speed rankings (PulsGdanska 2021/2022) agree closely with the project's speeds.

### Inferences
- **Tram speeds validate well.** Project 2026 values vs PulsGdanska 2022: Kraków 19.8 vs 19.2; Poznań 19.5 vs 19.9; Warszawa 18.6 vs 19.3; Gdańsk 18.0 vs 17.5; GZM 17.2 vs 17.9 ("GOP", presumably Tramwaje Śląskie); Łódź 16.9 vs 16.9; Wrocław 16.3 vs 16.7. Every city is within ±0.7 km/h, and Wrocław and Łódź are slowest in both. This is the best external check of the speed figure available, and it should be cited, with the caveat that the methods are unknown and four years apart.
- **Vehicle-km check (rough):** Wrocław 2023: about 48 m vehicle-km a year (29.3 + 4.5 + 14.1). Divided by 365 and by the city population (about 0.67 m per NSP 2021; population value taken from memory, not re-verified here), this gives about 0.19–0.20 per inhabitant per average day. The project's 0.249 is for a weekday, and weekday supply is typically above the annual daily average. They are therefore of the same order, but the size of the gap depends on how much higher weekdays are, on agglomeration buses outside the city, and on whether operator vehicle-km include dead runs. *Hypothesis, not verified:* Polish operators count metro (and possibly tram) "wozokilometry" per car, not per train, so Warsaw's ~280 m is not comparable with GTFS trip-km without converting train-km to car-km.
- BDL subjects P3365/P3603 (fleet operation, passenger transport) are at level 3, which in the BDL API level codes probably means a region-level aggregation rather than a city (code mapping not verified). Gmina-level (level 6) items are stops, bus lanes, P&R and route lengths. The project could compare route length (P3590) and active stops (P3826) per gmina against GTFS/OSM counts.
- Jakdojade's figures come from app usage, not network design, so they are not independent of timetables (the "wait" includes the time users spend before leaving home). The headway ranking (GZM worst at about 26 min; Wrocław and Warszawa best at about 13 min) agrees with the project's finding that GZM has the lowest share of residents near frequent service and the lowest transport performance.

- BDL variable lists and values for the seven cities were not retrieved (API rate limit). Registering for an API key (free, per GUS) was not done because the brief forbids using accounts.
- The PulsGdanska original report and method were not opened.

## 3. Published rankings and analyses (think tanks, NIK, institutes)

### Takeaway
The most relevant recent work is (a) the government-commissioned T-Included study of transport exclusion (four technical universities, published 12–13 March 2026, 21 sub-reports in a 105 MB ZIP); (b) NIK P/25/019 on urban PT in Warszawa, Kraków and Poznań with suburban gminas; (c) the Jakdojade 2025 route-query report; and (d) the Polityka Insight / FPPE sustainable-mobility city ranking (October 2025). No 2025–26 Klub Jagielloński or PIE report specific to these agglomerations was found.

### Cited Findings
- **T-Included ("Analiza skali wykluczenia komunikacyjnego…")**: consortium of Politechnika Poznańska (lead), Warszawska, Śląska and Gdańska; NCBR GOSPOSTRATEG, PLN 3.97 m, 2021–2025. Outputs include a public-transport database, algorithms for exclusion components, a composite risk indicator, a digital map and 21 sub-reports. Download: a single ZIP, "Raport końcowy oraz raporty cząstkowe" (104.95 MB). ### Inferences
- **Commuting matrix (highest value):** compute, for each FUA, the share of in-commuters to the core city from gminas inside vs outside the GTFS coverage mask. This tells users how many actual commuters the "incomplete coverage" flag affects, and it gives a flow-weighted transport-performance variant, for example the median PT time from each gmina to the core weighted by 2021 commuters. Car-based assumptions in the matrix do not matter for counting flows.
- **Age grid:** "share of residents aged 65+ (or 0–14) near frequent service" is a policy-relevant addition that needs no new routing. The 1 km resolution is the same as the RES grid already used.
- **BDOT10k buildings** (with residential function codes) could replace GHSL's built-volume as the weight for disaggregating census 1 km cells to 250 m. The project found GHSL misplaces people (RAPORT §15.2: 4.1% on the wrong side of cell edges after rescaling). Building footprints should place people better in new estates that are missing from GHSL 2020 (RAPORT §16.2 item 9).
- **RSPO + health facilities** as destinations would give "schools within 15/30 min" without relying on the modelled jobs grid, which covers only six cities and excludes GZM.
- GTFS-RT for Gdańsk, GZM, Warszawa (and Szczecin) would allow a "scheduled vs real" speed and headway check, which addresses limitation 1 in RAPORT §16.2. Collecting it requires a logger running for at least one week.

## 5. GTFS sources: official publishers, issues, national access point (KPD)

### Takeaway
Poland's National Access Point for multimodal travel information is still officially "temporary": a quarterly-updated table of data holders on dane.gov.pl (dataset 1739) plus a national NeTEx profile, latest version 11.0 dated 16.09.2026. It does not host GTFS feeds. A permanent KPD is being built as a separate project. Feed discovery therefore still relies on publishers' own pages, community repackagers (mkuran.pl and others) and the Mobility Database or Transitland catalogues, as the project already does.

### Cited Findings
- The KPD MMTIS is described by the Ministry of Infrastructure as a "tymczasowy Krajowy Punkt Dostępu". Data-holder information is published as a table on [dane.gov.pl dataset 1739](https://dane.gov.pl/pl/dataset/1739), updated quarterly (now with a "kategoria danych" column). A national NeTEx profile is attached. Data holders send data to the ministry using a template. First publication 29.11.2019; version 11.0 dated 16.09.2026. Legal basis: Delegated Regulation (EU) 2017/1926 as amended by 2024/490 (consolidated text 4 March 2024). A separate project page covers the final KPD — [gov.pl KPD MMTIS](https://www.gov.pl/web/infrastruktura/krajowy-punkt-dostepu-do-uslug-informacji-o-podrozach-multimodalnych-kpd-mmtis).
- The Polish NeTEx profile is described as compatible with XML, CSV and GTFS (GeoJSON) outputs (search summary); a 2019 ministry questionnaire asked data holders whether they would supply GTFS static or GTFS-RT to the temporary KPD from 1 December 2019 — [gov.pl attachments](https://www.gov.pl/attachment/b05761b9-5b6b-4ce7-9473-ef6b4bac804b) (search snippet).
- In January 2024 Poland had not set up a working NAP (secondary) — [Spidersweb, Jan 2024](https://spidersweb.pl/2024/01/rozklady-jazdy-dostep-w-polsce.html). A January 2026 document of the Związek Powiatów Polskich still calls the lack of a synchronised national access point a basic problem — [ZPP PDF](https://zpp.pl/storage/library/2026-01/3bbf2da4bf2f16c6deffd6ffe787e11a.pdf) (search snippets).
- Official city feeds and their known defects are documented in the project (RAPORT §2.2): 14 official and 28 unofficial feeds. Defects include GZM duplicate trips (25% on the reference day), Kraków service_ids with no trips on Thursdays and Fridays, and Łódź stops at (0,0).

### Inferences
- Because there is no national GTFS hub, the project's per-feed provenance table and its "official only" vs "all feeds" variants are the right approach. The KPD table on dane.gov.pl can be used to check which suburban carriers have registered as data holders (and so may be obliged to publish), to target feeds still missing (PKS Grójec, MZK Pabianice, PKS Gdynia and others, RAPORT §6.4 and §6.6).
- *From prior knowledge, not verified in this session:* the amended Delegated Regulation 2024/490 phases in obligations for static data (scheduled services on the comprehensive TEN-T network first, then the whole network, with deadlines in the mid to late 2020s). If so, more official suburban feeds should appear by about 2026–2028. Check the regulation text before stating this to users.

### Gaps
- The contents of dane.gov.pl dataset 1739 (which publishers, which formats) were not opened.
- No independent quality review of Polish official GTFS was found beyond the project's own validator runs.

## 8. Recommendations: cross-checks, additional measures, context for users (objective c)

### Takeaway
The cheapest high-value steps are: cite the PulsGdanska tram-speed ranking as external validation; add the Gdańsk GBR per-mode trip times and the Jakdojade transfer and headway rankings as context; run the 15 NIK suburban PT-vs-car pairs through R5; and add age-group and commuting-flow weighting from GUS NSP 2021 open data.

### Cited Findings
- Tram-speed benchmark (2021/2022) — [Antyweb / PulsGdanska](https://antyweb.pl/predkosc-tramwajow-w-polsce).
- Per-mode trip durations (Gdańsk 2022: PT 36, car 25, bike 21, walk 13 min) — [GBR Raport 3a p. 37](https://www.brg.gda.pl/attachments/article/1905/Raport-3a---opracowanie-wynikow-badan-i-pomiarow.pdf).
- PT-vs-car times for 15 suburban gminas (NIK P/25/019) — [NIK](https://www.nik.gov.pl/komunikaty/zamiast-samochodu-autobus-tramwaj-i-pociag-jak-miasta-zachecaja-do-korzystania-z-transportu-publicznego/).
- Transfer, headway, wait and punctuality rankings from app data (October 2025) — [Jakdojade](https://www.wroclaw.pl/beta2/files/dokumenty/968637/Jakdojade-raport-2026-Miasta-w-ruchu.pdf).
- Commuting matrix NSP 2021 — [GUS](https://stat.gov.pl/spisy-powszechne/nsp-2021/nsp-2021-wyniki-ostateczne/macierz-przeplywow-ludnosci-zwiazanych-z-zatrudnieniem-nsp-2021,9,2.html); age grid — [GUS geoportal](https://portal.geo.stat.gov.pl/en/news/2021-census-data-residents-in-1-km-grid-in-division-by-sex-and-age-groups/).
- Operator vehicle-km (Wrocław 2023) — [wroclaw.pl](https://www.wroclaw.pl/komunikacja/komunikacja-miejska-we-wroclawiu-i-aglomeracji-200-mln-pasazerow).

### Inferences
1. **Speed check (done by desk, no computation):** add a table to RAPORT §15.3 with PulsGdanska 2022 values next to the project's 2026 values (all within ±0.7 km/h; same slowest cities).
2. **Supply check:** convert annual operator vehicle-km (Wrocław 2023, MPK Poznań 2024, ZTM Warszawa 2024/25 once the PDF is found) to weekday vehicle-km using the GTFS ratio of weekday to annual service (computable from the feeds' calendars). Compare per city with the project's 0.20–0.25 vehicle-km per inhabitant per day, after settling train-km vs car-km for metro and coupled trams.
3. **Suburban accessibility check:** route the NIK origin gminas (gmina seat → core city centre, morning peak) in R5 and compare with NIK's PT times (25–59 min). This is a check on the "obszar pokryty" results where the project is weakest.
4. **Context panel for users:** for each city, show the latest survey PT share with its definition and year (Wrocław 21.6% all trips 2024; Gdańsk ~28% 2022/23; Poznań 33.7% 2018; Kraków 54% non-walking 2023; Warszawa 40.6% / 23.6% by destination zone 2024/25; Łódź and GZM none). Show the Gdańsk per-mode trip times as a reminder that a typical PT trip takes about 36 minutes.
5. **New measures:** (a) % of residents aged 65+ and 0–14 near frequent service (NSP 2021 age grid); (b) commuter-weighted PT time from each FUA gmina to the core (NSP 2021 matrix); (c) schools reachable within 15/30 min (RSPO), independent of the jobs grid.
6. **Transfer layer:** compare the project's stop-area transfer walking times with Jakdojade's per-city order (Warszawa longest at 55 m, Wrocław shortest at 20 m). Expect level differences because of definitions, but the order should match.
7. **Future checks:** GTFS-RT logging (Gdańsk, GZM, Warszawa) for scheduled-vs-real speed; Łódź and Poznań KBR results expected in 2027–2028; GDDKiA "Ruch Drogowy 2025" for car-traffic context.

### Gaps
- None of these recommendations has been tested; the Jakdojade metric definitions, NIK's routing method and the operator vehicle-km definitions need confirming before use.
